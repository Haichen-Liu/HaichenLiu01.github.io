The hidden-view prompt usually carries a (subject:1.2) weight boost to help it survive the transform. Faces (Einstein, Marilyn Monroe, Van Gogh, generic elderly portraits) and Geng's "flexible scenes" (kitchens, houseplants, wine and cheese, bowls of fruit) are the most reliable hidden subjects. flip_h is excluded — three pilot pairs failed in latent space (see View transforms panel below).

Style tips (Geng et al. notebook)

  • Prefer an oil painting of or a pencil sketch of — these styles give the model freedom, so anagrams emerge more easily.
  • Avoid a photo of — photographic realism constrains the model too tightly.
  • Faces work very well as hidden subjects because the human visual system is specially tuned for face recognition (Einstein, Marilyn Monroe, old man, etc.).
  • Flexible scenes (a kitchen, houseplants, wine and cheese) outperform rigid single objects.
  • Keep subjects easily recognizable — abstract concepts ("freedom", "hope") tend to fail.
  • Weighted emphasis like (subject:1.2) helps strengthen the hidden view.
  • Two-view anagrams already work; 3- and 4-view illusions are considerably harder.
  • Intuition is unreliable — what feels easy can fail, and vice versa. Exploration is the only way.

Pixel-only transforms not applicable in latent space (pixel coordinate alignment is not preserved by the VAE): negate, skew, patch_permute, pixel_permute, inner_circle, square_hinge, jigsaw. These appear in Geng et al.'s pixel-space notebook but cannot be evaluated meaningfully here.

2. Quantitative figures — charts and plots

Figure 2. Headline ablation result across 4 methods (n=35 = 5 seeds × 7 cases). Four aggregate metrics: CLIP_Avg (higher better), Balance_Gap aggregate |mean(V1) − mean(V2)| (lower better), Balance_Min (higher better), Robust_Score (higher better). Reverse weighting mainly reallocates fidelity from View1 to View2; CLIP_Avg is not significantly changed. +Laplacian alone has little aggregate benefit; Full does not clearly outperform +Reverse.

Figure 3. Rebalancing trade-off across methods (n=35). View1 (canonical) line drops, View2 (hidden) line rises, average remains comparable. View1 and View2 changes are paired-significant (p = 3.58e-05 and p = 0.00095 respectively); CLIP_Avg change is not significant.

Figure 4. Pixel vs Latent per-case comparison at seed=42 (n=1 per case per method). Cross-regime reading rule: absolute CLIP_Avg scores are not directly comparable across pixel and latent because SD v1.5 uses CLIP ViT-L/14 as its text encoder while DeepFloyd IF uses T5-XXL. Balance_Gap is a within-image differential, so encoder-evaluator bias largely cancels; use it only as a sanity check for failure directions, not as a benchmark ranking.

Figure 5. Reverse-weight sweep on a preliminary n=30 calibration subset, with three candidate weight pairs [0.45/0.55, 0.40/0.60, 0.35/0.65]. [0.40, 0.60] was selected as a balance-oriented operating point among the swept candidates. This is a calibration choice, not a global-optimality claim; the selected weight is then fixed for the main n=35 ablation.
